## Comparative model

Adult *Acomys* and *Mus* receive the same sciatic nerve injury, and the tissue is then compared by
histology and immunostaining. Alongside the injury model, I culture dorsal root ganglion explants
from both species in three dimensions, which isolates early cellular behaviour from systemic
influence and makes outgrowth architecture directly observable.

## DRG explant phenotype

Adult *Acomys* DRG explants developed a radially organized, scaffold-like outgrowth architecture
distinct from the more dispersed morphology of *Mus* explants. The primary difference was spatial
organization rather than neurite growth alone.

The result identifies an architectural phenotype. The cellular and extracellular mechanisms that
generate that organization remain open questions.

## Experimental work

I designed and ran the comparative injury experiments and performed the histology behind them: H&E,
Masson's trichrome, and immunohistochemistry across more than 20 tissue samples. I optimized
whole-mount immunostaining by adjusting permeabilization, solvent conditions, and incubation times to
improve antibody penetration and reproducibility, and I established and maintained more than 50
primary culture preparations, including the DRG explants. Neurites were labelled with RT97 and
β-tubulin and non-neuronal structural cells with vimentin; I quantified explant morphology in ImageJ
and QuPath with statistical analysis in R.

## Output

Luo, Y., & Maden, M. *Adult Acomys DRG Explants Exhibit a Radially Organized Neurite Architecture
Distinct from Mus Explants.* University of Florida Journal of Undergraduate Research, accepted for
publication, 2026. Earlier stages of the comparison were presented as posters at the UF Department of
Biology Undergraduate Honors & Research Symposium (2025) and at the APSA x CURBS and UF Spring
Research symposia (2025).
