## Research question

The central question was whether distinct routes of KRAS suppression impose different evolutionary
pressures. Rather than treating recurrence as one endpoint, the analysis asked which lineages
persisted across treatment histories, which patterns were route-specific, and which conclusions
remained stable when the analysis assumptions changed.

## Analysis design

The barcode dataset contained more than 400,000 raw sequences, so the main analytical challenge was
not simply finding large clones. I developed a prioritization framework that combined recurrence,
abundance, effect size, treatment specificity, trajectory pattern, and robustness. I also connected
the lineage-tracing analysis with quantitative proteomic profiles spanning 6,851 unique proteins in
human PDAC parental, sensitive, and resistant states.

## My role

- Developed the clone-prioritization and robustness framework.
- Built the R analysis for clonal composition, trajectories, sensitivity testing, and visualization.
- Contributed to the experimental system through genotyping and PCR validation, cell-line work,
  dissections, and MRI tumor assessment.

## Experimental grounding

Working with both the data and the biological system changed how I evaluated a result: a clonal
pattern was only useful if it survived reasonable analytical perturbations and remained interpretable
in the context of how the model and phenotype were generated.

## Status

Presented as a poster and lightning talk at MD Anderson Cancer Center, 2026. The study is
unpublished, so clone-level results and treatment-group figures are not shown here.
